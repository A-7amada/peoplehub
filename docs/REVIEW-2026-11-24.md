# PeopleHub BRD , Professional Review

I reviewed the full BRD version 2.0, including the seven development rounds, functional requirements, workflow engine, performance management, security, infrastructure, testing, and operational limitations.

My assessment is that **PeopleHub is a strong HRMS demo with good technical and functional coverage**\. It goes beyond a basic employee management system, particularly in configurable workflows, performance management, authorization, and operational documentation.

However, I see several important gaps if the objective is to evolve it into an enterprise-grade HRMS or present it as a professional business requirements document.

I will review it from three perspectives

1. Business Analyst , completeness and business process coverage
2. HRMS Solution Architect , domain design and functional architecture
3. Enterprise Software Architect , security, scalability, reliability, and maintainability

## 3\. Functional gaps and recommended improvements

I reviewed each major HRMS module. These are the improvements I would prioritize.

### 3.1 Employee Management

(High priority)

The employee module covers personal information, employment information, contracts, organizational structure, and employment history. 

However, I recommend adding the following requirements

| Feature | Recommended improvement | Priority |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Employee lifecycle | Define onboarding and offboarding processes | High |
| Employee documents | Document repository with expiration alerts | High |
| Employee dependents | Manage spouse and children information | Medium |
| Employee status | Define effective dates and transition rules | High |
| Job history | Track historical department, manager, grade, and salary | High |
| Probation | Track probation periods and confirmation | Medium |
| Employee transfer | Support temporary and permanent transfers | High |
| Employee termination | Define clearance and asset handover | High |
| Custom fields | Allow HR to configure additional employee fields | Medium |

**Important business rule to add**

An employee transfer should not overwrite historical employment information.

For example, if an employee moves from Finance to IT on December 1

- Previous department remains recorded until November 30
- New department becomes effective December 1
- Historical attendance and payroll records remain associated with the correct department
- Reporting must support historical organizational structures

I strongly recommend introducing effective-dated employee assignments rather than maintaining only the current employee department and manager.

### 3.2 Attendance Management

(High priority)

Your attendance requirements cover shifts, grace periods, overtime, absence, corrections, and biometric integration. 

The missing part is the complexity of real-world attendance scenarios.

Recommended additions

| Feature | Business requirement |
|--------|--------|
| Multiple shifts | Support rotating and changing shifts |
| Overnight shifts | Attendance can start on one day and end on another |
| Missing punch | Automatically identify missing check-in or check-out |
| Multiple punches | Define how intermediate punches are treated |
| Flexible working | Support flexible working hours |
| Remote attendance | Define permitted locations and geofencing |
| Attendance locking | Lock approved attendance periods |
| Manual adjustments | Require reason and maintain original records |
| Holiday calendars | Support calendars per branch or employee group |
| Grace periods | Configurable by shift or employee category |

**One important architectural recommendation**

Separate raw attendance transactions from calculated attendance records.

**Recommended attendance processing**

**Raw Punch Transactions**

Device / Web / Manual

**Attendance Processing Engine**

Shift matching · Punch pairing · Exceptions

**Daily Attendance Summary**

Working hours · Late minutes · Absence · Overtime

**Payroll Integration**

This separation is important because biometric devices may send duplicate or delayed transactions. You should never lose the original punch when recalculating attendance.

### 3.3 Leave Management

(High priority)

The current requirements define annual, sick, casual, and unpaid leave with balance validation and approval. 

I recommend expanding the business rules

1. Leave accrual based on employment date or monthly entitlement.
2. Leave eligibility based on employee category and service duration.
3. Half-day and hourly leave.
4. Carry-forward expiration rules.
5. Leave balance adjustment with audit history.
6. Leave encashment.
7. Leave cancellation after approval.
8. Leave during notice period.
9. Negative balance policy.
10. Leave calendar by department.

**A particularly important scenario**

An employee submits a leave request for 10 days.

The manager approves it.

Before the leave starts, the employee cancels the request.

The system must

- Reverse the reserved or deducted balance correctly
- Cancel pending approval tasks if applicable
- Notify relevant stakeholders
- Record the cancellation reason
- Recalculate the leave balance without creating duplicate transactions

I would also recommend maintaining a Leave Transaction Ledger instead of relying only on a mutable balance field.

### 3.4 Payroll Management

(Critical priority)

This is the module that needs the most business analysis before any production implementation.

Your current payroll covers salary components, deductions, loans, tax, insurance, approval, bank exports, and payslips. 

But a production payroll system needs substantially more rules.

| Area | Missing requirement |
|--------|--------|
| Salary structure | Salary structures by employee grade and category |
| Payroll eligibility | Define who is included in each payroll cycle |
| Proration | Calculate salary for joining and termination during a month |
| Retroactive payroll | Handle salary increases effective in previous periods |
| Variable compensation | Bonuses, commissions, incentives |
| Deductions | Define deduction priority and statutory limits |
| Loans | Early repayment, cancellation, rescheduling |
| Payroll adjustments | Adjustment against previous payroll periods |
| Payroll locking | Prevent unauthorized modifications after approval |
| Reconciliation | Compare payroll results with bank payment results |
| Accounting | Generate journal entries by cost center |
| Final settlement | Define complete settlement components and approval |

**My strongest recommendation is to introduce a payroll calculation snapshot.**

Once a payroll cycle reaches final approval, the system should preserve

- Employee salary at calculation time
- Applicable salary components
- Attendance calculation results
- Applicable deductions
- Tax and insurance rules and their versions
- Calculation timestamp
- Calculation version

This guarantees that changing an employee's salary or attendance later does not silently change an already approved payroll.

Also introduce a Payroll Adjustment process instead of editing historical payroll records directly.

One more point: your document has two different descriptions of Egyptian tax and insurance calculations, one explicitly illustrative and another using 2026 parameters. These need to be reconciled into a single, clearly versioned payroll policy. Neither should be treated as legally validated without review by a qualified Egyptian payroll specialist.  

### 3.5 Approval Workflow Engine

(Strong foundation)

This is one of the strongest parts of the design.

You already support configurable approval steps, manager-based approval, role-based approval, conditional steps, delegation, and historical workflow snapshots.  

I would add these capabilities

| Feature | Description |
|--------|--------|
| Approval SLA | Define expected processing time for each step |
| Escalation | Escalate overdue requests to another authority |
| Parallel approvals | Allow multiple approvers to act simultaneously |
| Approval quorum | Define whether all or one of the approvers must approve |
| Dynamic approvers | Resolve approvers using employee hierarchy or department |
| Workflow versioning | Keep an explicit version for every workflow |
| Reassignment | Allow authorized reassignment of pending tasks |
| Recall request | Allow employees to recall requests before final approval |
| Substitute approver | Handle manager absence and unavailable approvers |
| Approval comments | Support mandatory comments for specific decisions |

**Important distinction**

Your current role-based step says that any user holding one of the specified roles can approve.

This is a valid approval model, but you should explicitly distinguish between

- Any one approver can approve
- All assigned approvers must approve
- A configured number of approvers must approve

These are different business behaviors and should not be implicitly determined by the workflow implementation.

### 3.6 Performance Management

(High priority)

Your performance module is already considerably more advanced than a basic appraisal module. It supports goals, weighted KPIs, self-assessment, manager evaluation, calibration, 360 feedback, competency templates, and development plans. 

I recommend the following next steps

- Define a competency framework by job grade.
- Support multiple performance review cycles.
- Define employee acknowledgement of published results.
- Introduce a formal performance appeal process.
- Allow HR to configure rating scales and classifications.
- Define confidentiality rules for 360 feedback.
- Introduce performance improvement plans with milestones.
- Track historical performance trends.

One important consideration is the 360-degree evaluation.

You currently disclose reviewer identities to HR and managers, while employees see only the average. 

I recommend defining separate visibility policies for reviewer identity, individual comments, and aggregate scores. Consider minimum reviewer thresholds before publishing aggregate feedback.

## 5\. Security and authorization improvements

(Critical priority)

Your document already includes role-based permissions, data scope, audit logging, password hashing, antiforgery, TOTP, and optimistic concurrency.   

However, HR systems contain highly sensitive information.

I would add an explicit security requirements section.

| Requirement | Recommendation |
|--------|--------|
| Field-level authorization | Restrict access to national ID, salary, bank account, and medical documents |
| Separation of duties | Payroll creator cannot be the sole final payroll approver |
| Session management | Session expiration, revocation, and device/session management |
| Data encryption | Encryption at rest for sensitive information and secrets |
| Audit integrity | Prevent ordinary administrators from modifying audit history |
| Failed login protection | Lockout and suspicious activity detection |
| Data privacy | Define data retention, deletion, and access policies |
| Attachment security | Malware scanning, file signature validation, and private storage |
| Backup security | Encrypted backups and controlled restoration |
| Administrative actions | Require stronger authorization for sensitive operations |

### A particularly important recommendation

Do not rely on role permissions alone.

Introduce three authorization dimensions

1. Functional permissions , What can the user do?
2. Data scope , Which employees can the user access?
3. Field visibility , Which employee information can the user see?

For example, an HR specialist might have permission to view all employees but should not necessarily be allowed to view every employee's bank details or salary.

Also, I would explicitly define whether the application supports multiple companies or legal entities.

This is important because multi-company HRMS requirements affect employee identity, organizational structure, payroll, reporting, and authorization throughout the system.

## 6\. Architecture recommendations

Your four-layer architecture is a reasonable starting point for a modular monolith. 

I would not recommend immediately moving to microservices.

Instead, strengthen the modular monolith around business domains.

**Recommended logical architecture**

**Presentation Layer**

MVC / REST API / Employee Self-Service

**Application Layer**

Use Cases · Authorization · Validation · Orchestration

**Domain Modules**

**Infrastructure Layer**

EF Core · Database · Cache · File Storage · Email · Background Jobs

### Specific technical recommendations

| Area | Recommendation |
|--------|--------|
| Domain boundaries | Separate payroll, attendance, leave, and employee domain responsibilities |
| Database | PostgreSQL or SQL Server for production |
| ORM | EF Core with explicit transaction boundaries |
| Background jobs | Use a durable job scheduler for payroll and notifications |
| Caching | Distributed cache for multi-instance deployments |
| File storage | Object storage rather than database blobs |
| Integration | Outbox pattern for reliable event publishing |
| Concurrency | Optimistic concurrency and database constraints |
| Reporting | Read-optimized queries or reporting projections |
| Observability | Structured logging, metrics, tracing, and health checks |

### Five design patterns I recommend

These are not mandatory additions. They address specific problems in your existing requirements.

1. **Strategy Pattern** , Payroll calculation policies and attendance calculation policies.
2. **Specification Pattern** , Employee eligibility, leave validation, and approval conditions.
3. **State Machine Pattern** , Payroll lifecycle and performance review lifecycle.
4. **Outbox Pattern** , Reliable email notifications and integration events.
5. **Domain Events** , Employee transferred, leave approved, payroll finalized, employee terminated.

For example, payroll calculations should not be tightly coupled to the payroll controller or employee management module.

You should be able to introduce a different payroll policy without modifying the entire payroll process.

## 7\. Non-functional requirements need measurable targets

This is one of the biggest weaknesses in the current document.

The existing NFR section identifies security, language, performance, quality, deployment, and maintainability, but most requirements are descriptive rather than measurable. 

I suggest introducing the following targets as proposed acceptance criteria, subject to business agreement.

| Category | Proposed requirement |
|--------|--------|
| Performance | 95% of normal requests complete within 2 seconds |
| Availability | 99.9% monthly availability |
| Scalability | Support at least 1,000 concurrent users initially |
| Recovery | RPO of 15 minutes and RTO of 4 hours |
| Security | No unresolved critical or high-severity vulnerabilities before production |
| Database | Automated daily backups and verified restoration |
| Audit | Record all sensitive business operations |
| Localization | Full Arabic RTL and English LTR |
| Accessibility | Target WCAG 2.2 AA |
| Compatibility | Support agreed current versions of major browsers |

These numbers are proposed targets, not requirements already established by the document.

You should also distinguish between

- Performance under normal conditions
- Performance during payroll processing
- Bulk employee imports
- Report generation
- Concurrent approval processing

A system that responds quickly to an individual employee request might still struggle when processing payroll for thousands of employees.

## 10\. Improvements to acceptance criteria

Your current acceptance criteria focus heavily on permissions, workflows, localization, and successful tests. 

I recommend making them scenario-based.

For example, instead of

> The system calculates employee salary based on attendance.

Use the following structure

**Requirement: Payroll calculation**

- Given an employee with an approved attendance record
- And a defined salary structure
- And applicable deduction policies
- When the payroll cycle is generated
- Then the system calculates the employee's gross salary, deductions, and net salary
- And records the calculation inputs
- And prevents unauthorized modification after payroll finalization

You should also define acceptance criteria for exceptional scenarios.

Examples

- Duplicate attendance transactions must not generate duplicate attendance records.
- An employee transferred during a payroll period must receive the correct salary allocation.
- A failed bank export must not change the payroll status to paid.
- A rejected payroll cycle must preserve its previous calculation history.
- A terminated employee must not appear in subsequent payroll cycles unless explicitly included for settlement.

This will significantly improve the quality of development stories and testing.

## 12\. Additional requirements I would add to the BRD

There are four cross-cutting capabilities that I think deserve their own sections rather than being buried inside individual modules.

**A. Multi-company and multi-branch management**

Define whether the system supports multiple legal entities, shared employees, intercompany transfers, different payroll policies, and independent approval structures.

**B. Data migration**

Define how existing employee data, historical attendance, leave balances, salary history, and payroll records are imported from legacy systems.

Include validation, duplicate detection, reconciliation, and rollback.

**C. Integration management**

Define an integration framework with

- API authentication
- Retry and failure handling
- Idempotency
- Integration logs
- External reference IDs
- Reconciliation
- Versioning

**D. Data governance**

Define data ownership, retention, archival, employee data access, correction requests, and sensitive information handling.

These are essential for an HRMS that will eventually manage real employee information.

## 13\. Final verdict

My assessment is that PeopleHub has a good functional foundation and demonstrates strong engineering decisions, especially around configurable approvals, performance management, security improvements, and database recovery.

However, I would distinguish between three levels of maturity

| Level | Current assessment |
|--------|--------|
| HRMS demonstration | Strong |
| Business-ready HRMS specification | Needs refinement |
| Enterprise production HRMS | Requires substantial additional work and validation |

**My top five recommendations are**

1. Separate the formal BRD from implementation history and technical documentation.
2. Make payroll a properly specified and independently validated business domain.
3. Introduce historical employee assignments and transaction-based balances.
4. Strengthen attendance processing and exceptional business scenarios.
5. Establish measurable non-functional requirements and scenario-based acceptance criteria.

One final point: I would not recommend moving to microservices or introducing additional infrastructure just to make the architecture look more enterprise-oriented. A well-designed modular monolith is entirely appropriate for the current scope.
